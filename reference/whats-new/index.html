What’s new

Recent changes to InverseAudit.

November 2026

  • ASHRAE Level 2 report. On Energy Auditing, choose Report: ASHRAE Level 2 to add the pages ASHRAE Standard 211 asks for a Level 2 audit, including a compliance form, the facility and its systems, an equipment inventory, calibration results and more about each measure. A qualified energy auditor completes them in the new Site visit tab, and the report stays a draft until they’re done. Report: ASHRAE Level 1 gives the Level 1 report, with only the entries Level 1 asks for. See The ASHRAE Level 1 and Level 2 reports.
  • One table for each set of figures. The Level 2 report no longer repeats the same figures in two tables: the Analysis section opens with the end uses of every fuel and the End-use breakdown (now with each end use’s peak kW), the cost table gives the material, labor and markup, the savings by measure leave the totals to the QA/QC check, and the Utility analysis is the historical energy use, with the EUI and costs by year only for buildings with several years. See What the report adds.
  • Each level’s own measure summary. The Level 2 report no longer has Level 1’s measure summary with Low, Medium and High bands: it has the standard’s Level 2 form with each measure’s figures, at the top of Recommendations, and each measure’s comfort and other non-energy impacts are next to its O&M impact. See Measure text and priorities.
  • One measure table in every report PDF. The InverseAudit report’s Recommendations open with the same measure table as the Level 2 report, with each measure’s description, life, ROI and payback, low-cost and capital measures listed apart, and all measures together. The report PDFs no longer print the SIR; the Recommendations tab still shows it. See Reports and PDFs.
  • A shorter Level 1 report. The ASHRAE Level 1 report’s Analysis is the baseline’s energy use by end use, and its Recommendations are the measure summary, without InverseAudit’s measure-by-measure results, which are Level 2’s. See What Level 1 leaves out.
  • Bill costs in the ASHRAE reports. On the Historical energy use page, a bill without its cost on the meter data gets an estimate at the average rate (the year’s cost ÷ use at the building’s rates), in grey, instead of a blank and a $0 total. The interval data’s estimate and the Annual summary use the same rate. See What the report adds.
  • Standard 211 or not. When no program asks for ASHRAE Standard 211, untick This project must meet ASHRAE Standard 211 on the ASHRAE report’s Site visit tab (Compliance page). Every entry becomes optional, the report is never a draft, and it keeps its pages without naming the standard, claiming compliance or printing the compliance form. See If the project doesn’t need to meet Standard 211.
  • Report PDFs have one layout: every section’s tables have names on the left and numbers on the right, and the ASHRAE reports’ charts match the rest of the report. Natural gas is orange in every chart, the charts sit centred under their titles, and headings are in sentence case. The cover has no page header or footer, and its statement and signatures sit at the foot of the page. Each file’s name starts with the report type, such as ASHRAE Level 2. For the InverseAudit report, Download PDF also has Appendix B: Carbon impact of measures. See Reports and PDFs.
  • Report fixes. Charts in the report PDFs are always printed in full; on a busy server some used to print half-drawn or blank. Appendix A gives the building’s type, the energy data it covers and the calibration’s results, instead of fixed text. An ASHRAE report’s cover isn’t certified while the report is a draft. Report text written from older results, for example before new utility rates, is flagged on the Energy Audit page and when you post. See Calculate, update and post.
  • Benchmarking PDF. Download PDF on the Benchmarking page prints the Scores and End uses tabs with every table, chart and analysis. See Understand the benchmark.
  • The end-use score tables have a score shading key, on the page and in the PDF, and each fuel keeps its own color in the share of energy cost (electricity blue, natural gas orange). See Scenarios and the energy use gap.
  • The Typical Operation Scenario uses the schedules of the ASHRAE Standard 90.1-2007 User’s Manual for the building type, from the next calibration. See How scores are calculated.
  • When Benchmarking or Energy Auditing has no building to show, it says why (no building yet, none calibrated, or none pinned) and links to the next step on Home.
  • Rates from your bills. The AI bill import reads each bill’s total cost, and each rate becomes the bills’ average cost per unit. Check Inputs warns when a rate you entered is more than 25% off the bills. See Rates.
  • Natural gas. The calibration fits the year’s total gas use, so a month the utility skipped and billed with the next one does no harm: enter 0 for it. If your bills are actual meter readings, tick Bills are actual meter readings to compare each month instead. See Meter data.
  • Calibration. Hourly calibrations are scored on bias (NMBE) as well as CV(RMSE), as monthly ones are, and gas and fuel bills weigh at most 25% of the score. The search starts from the schedules and setpoints you entered. See Check inputs and run calibration.
  • Weather. The calibration uses the meter year’s measured cloud cover and station pressure along with temperatures, with the typical year’s solar radiation, and typical-year runs use TMYx 2011-2025 weather from the nearest station (CWEC in Canada). Baselines that already have a TMY3 typical year keep it.
  • Time. Meter data, weather and charts are in the building’s local standard time. Interval files recorded in clock time (with daylight saving) are converted automatically, and the model applies daylight saving to its schedules. Recalibrate hourly baselines to use this. See Supported data formats.
  • Green Button import reads the file’s units correctly (a 15-minute Wh file used to come in far too large), subtracts solar export from delivered energy, and uses electricity readings only.
  • Fuel oil bills are counted in gallons, purchased steam bills now count in the calibration, and chilled water and steam meter data are used in hourly calibrations whenever they’re uploaded.
  • A billing year that starts on February 29 now covers the full year.

October 2026

  • Benchmarking has two tabs, Scores and End uses, under one bar with the building and Run AI analysis, and each text says whether it is an AI analysis or the Standard text. See Understand the benchmark.
  • On Energy Auditing, the ECM Explorer is now the Measures panel, with one key for the colored dots, and the PDFs are under Download PDF at the top. See Run an energy audit and Reports and PDFs.
  • Tables have one Export menu (Copy, Print, Excel, PDF); Portfolio also has Columns.
  • Each person at a client company has their own Home tab. See What clients see.
  • End-Use Benchmarking now opens with the site EUI of the three compared scenarios and a new Energy Use Gap tab. See Scenarios and the energy use gap.
  • The Benchmark page shows the model fit as bars, with CV(RMSE) and NMBE against the ASHRAE Guideline 14 limits. See Read the calibration results.
  • The ENERGY STAR score uses the modeled floor area and is recalculated automatically when the model changes. See Scores.
  • Choose a calibration length: Quick, Standard or Thorough. See Check inputs and run calibration.
  • New accounts include a calibrated Demo Building to try the whole workflow. See Quick start with the Demo Building.
  • The Recommendations table shows the correct fuel oil, chilled water and steam savings for each measure.
  • Saving meter data without changes no longer sends a calibrated baseline back to preparation.

Recently added

  • AI utility bill import fills in meter data from your bills and interval data files. See Meter data.
  • AI insights explain the benchmark results, and AI-suggested ECMs rate the measures. See AI insights and the Measures panel.
  • AI Analyze rewrites the audit report’s text. See Calculate, update and post.
  • The ECM Explorer has an Electrification tab for replacing fossil-fuel heating. See ECM parameters.

January 2024

  • Monthly meter data is supported.
