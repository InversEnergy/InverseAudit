How scores are calculated

How InverseAudit calculates the operational score and the asset score: the three models it compares, what each one changes, the formula, the scores by fuel and end use, and the scores after the retrofit.

For: Everyone with access to the buildingWhere: Analysis › Benchmarking and Analysis › Energy Auditing

In short

Both scores compare your building with a version of itself, not with other buildings:

  • The operational score compares your building with the same building run the typical way: typical schedules and setpoints for its building type. It answers “is the building operated well?”
  • The asset score compares your building with the same building built to the energy code: code-level insulation, windows, lighting and equipment, on your own schedules. It answers “is the building itself efficient?”

Each score is 100 - 50 × your building's EUI ÷ the reference EUI. A score of 50 means your building uses as much energy as the reference, higher is better, and the scale runs from 0 to 100.

The three models

All three models start from your building’s calibrated model, and all three run with the same typical-year weather: TMYx 2011-2025 from the nearest weather station (CWEC in Canada), or TMY3 for buildings that already use it. Using a typical year takes the weather out of the comparison, so a hot summer or a mild winter doesn’t move the scores.

 ModelWhat it isWhat changes from your building
ACurrent building performanceYour calibrated model, run with typical-year weather (the weather-normalized model)Nothing
BTypical operation scenarioYour building operated the typical way for its building typeSchedules, setpoints and how the floors are used
CCode-compliant scenarioYour building built to the selected energy codeThe envelope, lighting, HVAC and water heating values the code sets

B changes only how the building is run, and C changes only what it’s built with, so each score isolates one side of your building’s performance.

A: Current building performance

The calibrated model is the model that matches your meter data with the meter year’s actual weather (see Check inputs and run calibration). For the scores, InverseAudit runs it again with the typical year’s weather. This is the weather-normalized model, and it’s the building “as it operates today” in every benchmark result.

B: Typical operation scenario

B is your building with its operation replaced by typical operation. The schedules come from the ASHRAE Standard 90.1-2007 User’s Manual (Appendix G, Table G-I, the schedules of Standard 90.1-1989) for your building type. Office, retail, school, hotel, hospital, warehouse and the other building types each have their own.

What B replaces:

  • Hourly schedules for weekdays, Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays: interior lighting, office equipment (plug loads), miscellaneous equipment, occupancy and domestic hot water use.
  • HVAC hours - When the fans run, from the same schedules.
  • Setpoints - 76 °F cooling and 72 °F heating while the HVAC runs, and 90 °F and 55 °F while it’s off.
  • Infiltration - Follows the typical fan hours.
  • How the floors are used - Every floor is occupied, lit and conditioned on the typical schedule. Floors you set as running 24/7, as having the lights off or as unconditioned are put back on it.
  • Dedicated outdoor-air units - Run only while the building is occupied.

What B keeps from your building: the geometry and envelope, the lighting and plug load densities, the HVAC system with its efficiencies and controls, the outside air, and the water heater. Only the way the building is run changes.

C: Code-compliant scenario

C is your building with every value the selected energy code sets replaced by the code’s value, for your climate zone, building type and HVAC system. These are the values of the Energy Code (blue) buttons in the Measures panel; see ECM parameters.

What C replaces, where your building has it:

  • Envelope - Roof and exterior wall insulation (R-value), window U-value and shading coefficient, and R-30 under a lowest floor over open parking.
  • Lighting - Lighting power density, and the occupancy sensor controls during occupied and unoccupied hours.
  • Air-side - DX cooling efficiency (EER), heat pump heating efficiency (COP), furnace efficiency, motorized outdoor-air dampers, minimum outside air, and dedicated outdoor-air units running only while occupied. The air-side economizer follows the code: integrated, with the code’s high limit, where the code requires one, and none where it doesn’t. For VAV systems also: a 30% minimum fan flow and box flow, supply air temperature reset where the code asks for it, and the ASHRAE fan part-load curve.
  • Chilled water plant - Chiller efficiency (kW/ton), 44 °F chilled water, the pump heads, and turning the chilled water off by outdoor temperature.
  • Hot water plant - Boiler efficiency, 180 °F hot water, the pump head, and turning the hot water off above 65 °F outdoors.
  • Water heater - Efficiency: 80% for gas, 96% for electric, and a COP of 3.6 for a heat pump.

What C keeps from your building: the schedules, setpoints and setbacks (your own operation), the plug and process loads, infiltration, the HVAC system type and capacities, fan operation, supply air temperatures and exhaust. A value the code doesn’t set stays as calibrated.

The energy code is the one selected on the Current performance vs. energy code card on Benchmarking; the code currently in force in your state is tagged Current State Code. Only the building’s analyzer can change it. Changing it runs C again and changes the asset score. See Scores.

The formula

Energy use intensity

Each model’s energy use intensity (EUI) is its site energy for the year, all fuels together, divided by the floor area:

EUI = (electricity + natural gas + other fuels, in kBtu) ÷ floor area (ft²)

Each fuel is converted to kBtu first: 3.413 kBtu per kWh, 100 kBtu per therm and 137.4 kBtu per gallon of fuel oil. The floor area is the same for all three models, so it cancels out of the scores: they compare total energy use.

The scores

Operational score = 100 - 50 × A EUI ÷ B EUI

Asset score = 100 - 50 × A EUI ÷ C EUI

50 × A ÷ reference is rounded to a whole number first. A building that uses twice the reference or more scores 0, so scores stay between 0 and 100. Only a building that uses no energy would score 100.

Example

An office building’s three models use, in kBtu/ft² a year:

A Current buildingB Typical operationC Code-compliant
8070100
  • Operational score = 100 - 50 × 80 ÷ 70 = 100 - 57 = 43. The building uses about 14% more than the same building run the typical way: it probably runs longer hours or with tighter setpoints than typical.
  • Asset score = 100 - 50 × 80 ÷ 100 = 100 - 40 = 60. On its own schedules, it uses 20% less than if it were built to code: its envelope and equipment are better than the code’s.

Reading the two scores together

The Whole-building performance score card’s standard text reads the pair this way:

 Operational score above 50Operational score below 50
Asset score above 50Performing well in both areas: keep it up with consistent operation and preventive maintenance.Solid building, operational issues: schedule and maintenance fixes can save quickly at low cost.
Asset score below 50Operated reasonably well, held back by outdated equipment and systems: capital upgrades have the biggest impact.Both need attention: start with low-cost operational fixes while planning equipment upgrades.

When either score is exactly 50, it reads as moderate performance across the board.

Scores by fuel and by end use

The End uses tab applies the same formula to parts of the building’s energy, in the End-use performance tables:

  • Each end use - Lighting, plug loads, heating, cooling, fans, pumps and the others, each against the same end use in B (operational score) and in C (asset score). Electric end uses are compared in kWh/ft², and natural gas and other fuels in kBtu/ft².
  • Each fuel - Electricity on its own and the other fuels together, and each energy source in the Energy use scores table.

A dash (–) means there’s no score: the reference uses none of that end use, so there’s nothing to compare with.

The score cells are shaded with the key above the tables: 80 and above, 50-79, 40-49, 30-39 and below 30. An end use with a low operational score points to its schedules or setpoints, for example fans or pumps running at night. One with a low asset score points to its equipment or the envelope. See Scenarios and the energy use gap.

The whole-building scores aren’t an average of the end-use scores: they compare the total energy, so the end uses that use the most count the most.

Scores after the retrofit

On Energy Auditing, the Score card shows a score now and after the measures. Under Manage, choose which one: ENERGY STAR score, ENERGY STAR site EUI, operational score or asset score. See Run an energy audit.

The scores after the retrofit use the same references, B and C, with your building after the measures in place of A. Each score counts only its own kind of measure:

  • Asset score after the retrofit - Your building with the envelope and equipment measures only: every measure except the Operations group. Outdoor-air dampers and controls count here, because they take capital.
  • Operational score after the retrofit - Your building with the Operations measures only: schedules, setpoints, setbacks, floors and the dedicated outdoor-air schedule.

This way a new setback doesn’t raise the asset score, and new windows don’t raise the operational score. Calculate runs these two models along with all the measures together. If there are no measures of one kind, that score stays as it is now. For a building whose measures were last calculated before November 2026, both scores use all the measures together until you click Calculate again.

The card’s note reads the score now: below 40 means significant savings opportunities, 40 to 59 a medium range that still has substantial ones, and 60 or more a building that is well operated (operational score) or well maintained (asset score).

When the scores change

  • After a calibration - All three models run again, so both scores can change.
  • When the energy code changes - C runs again for the new code, so the asset score changes.
  • When you calculate measures - Only the scores after the retrofit change.

The scores don’t use your bills directly: they compare models, all with the same typical weather. New bills change them only through a new calibration.

How they differ from the ENERGY STAR score

The ENERGY STAR score compares your building with similar buildings nationwide, using ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. It reflects the building, its operation and its use all together, and 50 is the national median.

The operational and asset scores compare your building only with versions of itself. That makes them work for building types ENERGY STAR doesn’t cover, and it tells you which side to work on: the operation or the building itself. As with the ENERGY STAR score, 50 is the middle and higher is better.
