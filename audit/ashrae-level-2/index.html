The ASHRAE Level 1 and Level 2 reports

Choose the ASHRAE Level 1 or Level 2 report when a program asks for an ASHRAE Standard 211 energy audit. It adds the pages the standard asks for at that level, and a qualified energy auditor completes them after a site visit.

Which report to use

All three reports come from the same calibrated model and the same calculations.

  • InverseAudit - The usual report. It needs no site visit, so it suits remote screening, owner reports and planning.
  • ASHRAE Level 1 - What ASHRAE Standard 211 asks for a Level 1 audit: a site visit, a description of the building, the historical energy use with the benchmark and target, and the measure summary of low-cost and capital measures, with InverseAudit’s executive summary and utility analysis. It leaves out InverseAudit’s measure-by-measure results, which are Level 2’s. Use it when a program asks for a Level 1 audit only: the auditor has far fewer entries to make (about a third as many).
  • ASHRAE Level 2 - Level 1 plus what the standard asks for a Level 2 audit: the building’s systems (envelope, HVAC, water heating, lighting and loads), an equipment inventory, the end use breakdown, and more about each measure and the package. Use it when a program asks for a Standard 211 Level 2 audit, for example C-PACE financing, LEED O+M or a city’s audit ordinance.

The standard doesn’t cover remote audits: every level needs a site visit by a qualified energy auditor. InverseAudit’s analysis alone isn’t a Standard 211 audit, which is why an ASHRAE report stays a draft until the auditor’s entries are done. When no program asks for the standard, see If the project doesn’t need to meet Standard 211.

Choose the report

  1. On Energy Auditing, choose the building.
  2. Next to Download PDF, click ASHRAE Level 1 or ASHRAE Level 2 under Report.
  3. Click Use ASHRAE Level 1 or Use ASHRAE Level 2 to confirm.

The page reloads with a Site visit tab, and the Download PDF menu lists the ASHRAE report’s parts. Each building keeps its own report type, and new buildings start on InverseAudit.

You can switch at any time. The site visit entries are shared by both levels, so moving from Level 1 to Level 2 keeps everything entered and adds Level 2’s entries; moving back keeps Level 2’s entries without printing them. Only the building’s analyzer can switch, and not while the audit is posted: unpost it first.

What Level 1 leaves out

At Level 1 the Site visit tab hides Envelope, Water heating, lighting, loads and Equipment inventory. HVAC asks only for the building automation, and Measures and package only for measures without calculated savings. Items to verify are optional, and Facility and contacts doesn’t ask for the building shape or the walls by facing.

The Level 1 report’s Analysis is only the baseline model’s energy use by end use, and its Recommendations are only the measure summary: InverseAudit’s before-and-after end-use analysis, monthly costs and use, measure tables, utility rates, savings table and measure write-ups are left out, because a Level 1 audit doesn’t calculate each measure. The Analysis and Recommendations PDFs are the same. The measures are still entered and calculated on the Energy Auditing page, and the measure summary uses their figures.

The Level 1 report also leaves out Level 2’s pages: Building envelope, HVAC systems, Water heating, lighting and other loads, Equipment inventory, the walls by facing, the End-use breakdown, Renewable and distributed energy, and Level 2’s Recommendations pages (the review with the owner, the cost breakdown, the Level 2 measure summary, the savings by measure and the QA/QC check, the package, M&V and Level 3). Its cover and compliance form say Level 1.

If the project doesn’t need to meet Standard 211

Some clients want the ASHRAE report’s pages without a Standard 211 audit, for example when the audit is remote. On the Site visit tab, open Compliance page and untick This project must meet ASHRAE Standard 211. The box is ticked unless you untick it.

With the box unticked:

  • Every site visit entry is optional. The entries you make are printed and empty ones aren’t, so the report is never a draft. Each section shows how many entries are made instead of how many are left.
  • Missing bills don’t make the report a draft. The historical energy use pages still say which bills are missing.
  • The report doesn’t name Standard 211. The cover says “Level 2 energy audit” (or Level 1), and so does the audit overview. The auditor’s statement certifies that the report is complete and correct, and mentions a site visit only if its date is entered. The tables keep the standard’s layout, but their notes don’t refer to its forms.
  • The compliance form (Form A) is left out, and so are the entries only it uses: the qualification route, the audits completed and, at Level 2, the Level 1 date. In Download PDF, Cover and compliance becomes Cover page.

You can tick the box again at any time, and nothing you entered is lost. Like the other entries, it locks while the audit is posted.

The Site visit tab

The qualified energy auditor fills in the Site visit tab after visiting the building. Its sections are listed on the left, each with how many of its entries are done:

  • Site visit and interviews - The dates, who was there, the areas and systems visited, what was checked, the interviews, operation and maintenance observations, and the main findings for the Executive summary.
  • Items to verify - What the owner entered and what the calibration found, for the operating hours, setpoints, lighting, heating, cooling, outside air and water heating. Tick Confirmed when the site visit agrees, or write what was found. A ticked item’s figures print as Modeled, verified instead of Modeled, verify. Recalibrating clears the ticks, since the calibrated values can change; what you wrote is kept.
  • Facility and contacts - Renovations, earlier audits, landmark status, the owner and bill payer, other space types, the occupancy schedule, and the walls on each side of the building.
  • Envelope, HVAC and Water heating, lighting, loads - What the model can’t know, such as construction, condition, controls and nameplates.
  • Equipment inventory - The units installed, one row for each unit or group of identical units.
  • Utility accounts and rates - Account and meter numbers, and the tariffs; and, when a utility isn’t calibrated, the Calibration follow-up (see When a utility isn’t calibrated).
  • Measures and package - The review with the owner, planned dates, reasons for measures not evaluated, funding, O&M and M&V costs, and the assessment of solar PV and combined heat and power.
  • Compliance page - Whether the project must meet ASHRAE Standard 211, the building owner, the auditor and their qualification, and the dates the Level 1 and Level 2 audits were completed.

Each entry saves when you leave it. For tables, click Add a row to add a row and × to remove one. The entries lock while the audit is posted, and only the building’s analyzer can change them. The number on the tab is how many entries are still empty.

An entry left empty isn’t printed, and neither is its title. A table or column with nothing else in it is left out too. The exception is the compliance form (Form A): it keeps all its lines, blank where nothing is entered, so it can be filled in by hand.

Narratives

Envelope, HVAC and Water heating, lighting, loads each have narratives for describing the building as found, for example “Systems installed” for the HVAC. They’re optional, so a narrative left empty doesn’t keep the report a draft.

Measure text and priorities

The reports use InverseAudit’s text for each kind of measure: its comfort, other non-energy and O&M impacts and, at Level 1, the system it changes. Under Measures and package, the auditor can replace that text for this building, and at Level 1 a measure’s priority.

The measure summary with Low, Medium and High bands and a priority is Level 1’s form (L1 - EEM Summary). The Level 2 report has the standard’s Level 2 form instead (L2 - EEM Summary), with each measure’s savings, cost, life, ROI and payback. It opens the Recommendations section, as the same table does in the InverseAudit report, where only the non-energy cost savings column is left out. The comfort and other non-energy impacts are in the Level 2 report’s Other impacts and planned dates table.

Each figure is marked with where it comes from: Model (the building’s inputs and model), Modeled, verify (estimated by the calibration, for the auditor to check), Modeled, verified (ticked Confirmed under Items to verify), Calculated, or Auditor.

Equipment as the model’s totals

The calibrated model simplifies the building’s geometry and systems; for example, it may have one air handler per floor. So the report gives equipment as the model’s totals, such as the total cooling capacity, supply air and fan power. The auditor lists the units actually installed in the equipment inventory, and the report adds up their nameplate capacities next to the model’s totals.

When a utility isn’t calibrated

Sometimes the calibrated model can’t match a utility’s meter data, even though it reflects the building as documented. The report then says so, for the owner to check:

  • Natural gas or another fuel - when the year’s total of the bills and the model’s, over the same billing periods and with the same weather, differ by more than 5%.
  • Electricity - when the NMBE is outside the ASHRAE Guideline 14 limit: ±5% for monthly bills, ±10% for hourly data.

The audit overview’s Method names the utility, and the executive summary says what its bills show against the model, with finding out why as the first next step. Under Calibration results, a section for the utility lists its bills against the model, points out where most of the difference falls and any bill with an unusual period (under 20 or over 40 days), and gives the possible causes and what to check. The utility’s use, end uses and savings in the report are from the model. When the bills are higher than the model, the section also gives what the excess costs a year at the building’s average rate.

The Site visit tab then has an optional Calibration follow-up entry under Utility accounts and rates, for what was checked and the owner’s answer. It prints in that section.

Checks against the site visit and the bills

The report compares the model with what the site visit found and with the bills, and points out where they disagree, in dark orange. None of these checks changes the model: they tell the auditor what to look at.

Three site visit questions. Under HVAC and Water heating, lighting, loads, three optional choices record what a walk-through finds:

  • Outside air dampers when unoccupied - Close, Stay open or Not known. Both levels.
  • Water heater found on site - None, Electric, Gas or other fuel, Heat pump, or Not known. Both levels.
  • Economizer as found - Its changeover and whether it runs with the compressor. Level 2 only.

Differs notes. Under Items to verify, a model value that disagrees with the site visit gets a Differs: note, on the tab and in the PDF:

  • the dampers, the economizer or the water heater, against the answers above;
  • an equipment inventory whose cooling or boiler capacity is more than 20% from the model’s;
  • a gas furnace efficiency or a DX cooling EER below the federal minimum for the year the units were installed. The year comes from the equipment inventory, or else from the HVAC condition or a renovation that names the HVAC. Units under 65 kBtu/h have no federal EER and aren’t checked.

When the dampers are found to close already, the outdoor-air damper measure says so: Site visit: the dampers already close when unoccupied, so it may save nothing. Confirm or remove it.

Rates against the bills. In the utility rates table, each rate gets a line on how the bills compare with it, from at least 6 bills: when it fits, a fixed amount a bill plus a cost per unit, and how many bills come out more than 10% off their cost at the rate entered. For electricity with a flat rate, it also notes demand recorded on the bills with no demand charge, and the tariffs in InverseAudit’s rate database for the state. The measures are valued at the rate entered: to value them at what the bills show, enter the per-unit rate or the tariff in Rates and run the measures again.

Gas above the model. When the gas bills are higher than the model (see When a utility isn’t calibrated), the causes start with A gas leak, a safety item to check first. Each bill’s use a day is then compared with heating degree-days (base 65 °F): Heating runs more than documented when the bills follow the weather, A load that runs whatever the weather when they don’t, or both. A gas water heater found on site that the model doesn’t have is listed too.

Humid climates. In climate zones 0A to 3A, a setpoint measure with a cooling setback above 80 °F adds a caution to its comfort impact: humidity can build up over long closed periods. Text you changed yourself stays as you wrote it.

Report checks. The page header lists what to fix before the report goes out, for example ENERGY STAR score out of date, a completion date before the analysis, or a flat rate entered that’s far from the bills. A stale ENERGY STAR score is recalculated when you open Energy Auditing.

Measured first. The executive summary starts with what the bills show, then the model as the building as documented. The ENERGY STAR score and the costs are labelled as the model’s.

Draft until it’s complete

While any required site visit entry is empty, or the energy data is incomplete (for example, natural gas is used but there are no gas bills), the report is a draft:

  • Empty entries don’t print, so the Site visit tab is where to see which are open.
  • Every page header says DRAFT, with how many site visit items are open and what data is missing.
  • The compliance form shows Not yet for the standard’s requirements, and its signature lines are marked not to be signed.
  • Full report in the Download PDF menu shows how many items are open.

Enter 12 months of bills for each fuel on the building’s meter data to complete the energy data. See Meter data.

Download the PDFs

Under Download PDF, both ASHRAE reports have Full report, Cover and compliance, Executive summary, Site visit and facility, Analysis, Recommendations, Utility analysis and, for US buildings, Appendix A: ESG/carbon. The parts show the same pages as in the full report. The full report leaves out Appendix B (the carbon impact of each measure), which the standard doesn’t ask for; it stays in InverseAudit’s report and on the Recommendations tab. See Reports and PDFs.

What the auditor still decides

  • Measures without calculated savings - Keep each, with the reason, or drop it.
  • Measures not evaluated - Where the building is below the energy code and no measure was run, give the reason. A measure is listed as not recommended, with no reason needed, when the building already meets the code.
  • Priorities - They come from the payback. The cost, savings and ROI bands are InverseAudit’s, not values from the standard.
  • Solar PV and combined heat and power - The report sizes them and asks for the auditor’s assessment. Solar PV savings come from the Solar PV measure, when you run it; combined heat and power needs a Level 3 study.
  • Costs - Material, labor and markup come from the cost table’s shares for each kind of measure. A cost typed in on the Recommendations tab is shown as its total.

Check the standard’s own reporting forms and the program’s requirements before you submit the report.
